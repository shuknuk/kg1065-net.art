An online exhibition

What the
Browser Keeps

Curated by Kinshuk Goel

A browser is never only a window.

What the Browser Keeps brings together three works that make browsing feel less like a neutral way to reach information and more like a system that sorts, hides, and rearranges fragments. In My Boyfriend Came Back from the War, Olia Lialina turns a browser window into a fractured conversation. Each click divides the page further, so the reader moves through pieces of a relationship without getting a clean account of it. I/O/D's The Web Stalker takes a different route by replacing the usual browser with a visual map of links and data. It makes the web look like something to inspect instead of something that is already organized for the user. The Dumpster collects blog posts about breakups and turns them into an interface for moving through thousands of small personal records. Together, the works show that the browser frames what counts as a connection, what stays visible, and what is left as an unfinished trace.

01

My Boyfriend Came Back from the War

Olia Lialina · 1996

This nonlinear web narrative uses nested frames, linked text, and black-and-white images to break a conversation between two people into partial, competing paths. Each click divides the browser window, making the page structure part of the story.

02

The Web Stalker

I/O/D · 1997

This alternative web browser maps pages and links visually instead of presenting a conventional page-by-page experience. It makes browsing itself visible and questions the apparently neutral organization imposed by commercial browser software.

The Dumpster (Valentine's Day, 06)

Golan Levin, Kamal Nigam, and Jonathan Feinberg · 2006

This interactive visualization uses real blog posts about romantic breakups, letting visitors move through thousands of relationship fragments and patterns. It turns personal writing that might otherwise disappear into a navigable archive.

Three ways a browser holds on to what it has already broken apart.
